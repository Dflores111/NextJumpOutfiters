G01  Start here
A flatbed-first website that lets the buyer picture the finished truck, understand the choices, and reach a credible quote.
This version supersedes the earlier Website Blueprint for architecture, copy, and implementation guidance. It is a proposed build specification, not permission to publish or a verified product catalog. The live website was rechecked on October 3, 2026. A published claim is recorded as an observation, not automatically treated as an approved operational fact.
Use the existing Shopify site as the first implementation option. A redesign does not require a new platform. The same content and interaction contracts can support a rebuild elsewhere, but migration must preserve URLs, commerce rules, CRM capture, and maintainability. Do not start a replatforming project merely to obtain better-looking pages.

The commercial outcome
Optimize for qualified flatbed opportunities, accepted quotes, and sold installations, not just form volume. Give ready buyers a fast path. Let uncertain buyers learn without making them identify every component first. Work and business customers get a practical route; they are not forced through adventure-oriented language.
Launch blockers are explicit. Prices, approved fitment, package recipes, warranty wording, fulfillment, and booking promises must be confirmed by their named owner before they appear as facts. Null or unapproved data must never become zero dollars, “fits your truck,” “in stock,” or a reassuring invented promise.

G03  Positioning and the buyer’s story
The truck is the object. The purchase is about what the owner can do with it and how it feels to own.
Recommended positioning
A modular aluminum flatbed configured around your truck, with installation and the wider build discussed by the same Tacoma team. This is a proposed positioning direction, not a claim that every component is made in Tacoma or that every project can be delivered by the shop.
The story on the page
Buyer’s question
What the page should show
Could my truck look and work like that?
A real finished truck first, with enough context to identify the product.
Would it suit what I do?
Camper, overland, work, and business starting points in plain language.
What is different from the factory bed?
A factual visual comparison and short explanation of the platform.
What would I choose?
A small set of approved starting configurations, then compatible options.
Can I justify the price and trust the work?
What is included, the estimate basis, relevant completed builds, and clear written policies.
What happens after I reach out?
A specific inquiry/quote path, not a premature purchase or booking promise.

The emotional direction for personal buyers is anticipation and pride: the truck they have been imagining, a more usable trip setup, and decisions that feel like their own. Do not tell visitors they deserve debt, exaggerate urgency, or make the purchase feel like a test of identity. For work buyers, show everyday access and loading. A business buyer may care more about predictable scope than scenic aspiration.
Voice rules
Use ordinary truck-owner language, natural contractions, and useful labels. Avoid “ultimate,” “revolutionary,” “no limits,” and vague superlatives. Explain unfamiliar terms at the point of use. Keep the brand confident and welcoming; do not make newcomers feel like outsiders. One useful idea per block. The customer should never read an internal strategy label like “solution-aware.”
The page-copy book is newly written proposed copy. Technical, policy and product-specific statements still pass the approval gates shown for each page. Visuals must not imply capabilities that the accompanying copy carefully avoids promising.

Content layers
Layer 1 is the headline, photo, essential answer, and action. Layer 2 explains the option or comparison with a short caption or expanded detail. Headings and typography
Use sentence case for long headings and body copy. Brand styling may use restrained uppercase for small labels, not whole paragraphs. Use one clear H1 per page, meaningful H2 sections, and H3 for subtopics. Do not split readable words into animations that disrupt accessible names or initial visibility.
Copy and production notes are separate
In the Copy Book, CUSTOMER COPY is the public wording. VISUAL BRIEF, BEHAVIOR, and APPROVAL are internal instructions. The JSON follows the same separation. Never paste implementation notes, approval labels, placeholder fields, or internal source references into a customer page.

G10  Configuration rules, pricing, and saved state
The builder is a decision tool connected to real records, not a visual calculator with optimistic assumptions.
Fitment and engineering rules
Represent vehicle combinations explicitly: year range or generation, make/model/series, cab/wheelbase and bed as needed, single/dual rear wheel where relevant, fuel and vehicle-side variations, and documented exclusions. Nick owns getting qualified technical approval and the approved records. The implementer must not infer a valid installation from nominal dimensions, a product title, or a similar truck photo.
A rule can be approved, pending, or unsupported. Pending records may collect a help request; they may not display a green fit badge. No automatic camper compatibility from brand name. No computed vehicle payload based on a flatbed product’s load rating. Technical documentation is required for weights, mounting, and rated-load statements.
Pricing model
Store money as integer cents with currency, effective date, price version, approval state, and package/vehicle scope. Mark labor as included, separately estimated, or quoted separately. An installed price must not receive a second installation charge through the same line item. Taxes and any additional approved charges are shown on the same basis throughout.
Known-item subtotal = the sum of validated line quantities multiplied by their current unit amounts, plus separately estimated labor where applicable, minus an approved applicable discount. A total is not complete when a required item lacks a price. In that case show “One item still needs pricing,” identify the missing item, and call the number a priced-items subtotal. Never substitute zero.
Availability and changes
Inventory availability, installation capacity, and lead time are separate fields. “In stock” does not mean an install appointment is open. Preserve saved price snapshots for explanation, but recalculate from current records when reopened or submitted. Disclose any changed amount or unavailable component. Only use real, dated promotions with automatic expiry.
Dependencies and conflicts
An option requiring another paid component opens a confirmation with both components and the combined price change. Declining leaves the current build unchanged. Removing a prerequisite explains which options must be removed. Mutually exclusive alternatives are enforced in both client and server logic. Package edits recalculate inclusions and discounts consistently.
Saved and shared builds
Save an opaque configuration ID with a version, selection IDs, and validation state. A share link must not contain personal contact details, a VIN, or a customer’s private quote. Separate an anonymous inspiration configuration from a protected contact-linked record. Do not index saved-build URLs or put them in a sitemap. Explain that saving does not reserve inventory, pricing, or installation.
Server validation and resilient submission
Validate all pricing and fitment server-side; never trust client-supplied totals. Use idempotency keys so retries and double-clicks cannot create duplicate requests. Persist the inquiry before showing success. Send to HubSpot through a retryable, observable integration; a temporary CRM failure must not lose the customer request or ask them to submit twice. Do not put private API tokens in browser JavaScript.

G17  Accessibility and performance
A visual-led website must still be readable, operable, and fast on an ordinary phone.
Accessibility acceptance
Target WCAG 2.2 AA for the relevant interface. Test keyboard access, labeled fields, meaningful headings, visible focus, logical order, error identification, focus not covered by sticky UI, zoom/reflow, and non-color indicators. Use at least 44–48px touch targets as this project’s design target; WCAG 2.2’s minimum target-size criterion is 24px with stated exceptions, not a blanket 44px mandate. [S16,S17]
Make option groups labeled radio/checkbox controls or semantically equivalent components. Announce price/selection changes politely without overwhelming screen readers. A modal traps focus only while it is open and returns focus on close. Labels remain visible after typing. Show validation near the field and an error summary when appropriate. Preserve data after failed submission.
Performance goals
Use Core Web Vitals goals at the 75th percentile: LCP at most 2.5 seconds, INP at most 200ms, CLS at most 0.1. These are targets, not measurements of the current site. Field data is the acceptance evidence when available; lab testing helps diagnose before enough field data exists. [S22]
Proposed initial budgets: a responsive hero around 200–350KB where quality permits and route JavaScript around 200KB compressed before optional media/integrations. These are engineering starting budgets, not official thresholds. Test representative devices and adapt to actual behavior. Do not lazy-load the LCP hero; do defer below-fold media and click-load third-party video. Reserve image dimensions. Audit duplicate apps and third-party scripts.
Test matrix
At minimum test 360/390px mobile, 768px tablet, and 1,440px desktop; iOS Safari, Android Chrome, and desktop browser paths. Include keyboard-only, screen-reader sampling, 200% zoom and narrow reflow, reduced motion, slow network, disabled optional cookies, and form/CRM failure. Check embedded forms separately from the main page.
