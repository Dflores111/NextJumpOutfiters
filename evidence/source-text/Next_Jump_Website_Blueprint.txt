The decision
Keep Shopify and most of the relevant pages. Improve the buying journey before expanding the site.
Next Jump already has a flatbed hub, a camper-specific page, vehicle-specific builders, installation pages, customer proof, and a featured collaboration build. The opportunity is not to replace all of that. It is to make those assets work together, remove contradictory information, and help a customer move from interest to a build they understand. [S01–S08]
At launch, add only two new content destinations: Our Work and a Flatbed Buyer's Guide. Improve the existing camper page rather than creating another one. Keep overlanding and work as useful sections within the main flatbed page until there is enough distinct product information, customer evidence, and demand to justify dedicated pages.
Recommended positioning: A modular aluminum flatbed configured around your truck, with the installation and wider build handled by the same Tacoma team. This is a positioning recommendation, not a claim that Next Jump alone offers these benefits.
How to use this document
Part 1 identifies what to fix and why. Part 2 defines the sitemap and buying journey. Part 3 contains customer-facing copy for the core acquisition pages and reusable service templates. Part 4 covers implementation, measurement, and publication checks. Part 5 lists sources and the existing vehicle-page inventory.
Copy labels matter. Text after “Headline,” “Body,” “CTA,” or a named customer-facing section is proposed website copy. “Build note” and “Approval needed” are internal instructions, not content to publish. Bracketed fields such as [approved price] must be populated from a verified business source.
This is a website acquisition blueprint, not a rewrite of every retail SKU, historical blog post, or legal policy.
Part 1. What the current site gets right, and what needs work
1.1 The foundation is useful
The site already addresses campers, work, and overlanding. It has four flatbed sizes, a vehicle selector, accessory product listings, and relevant installation services. The camper page distinguishes slide-in campers, toppers, and purpose-built flatbed campers. The Super Ute page provides an actual truck, named collaborators, and a documented configuration. These are assets to improve, not gaps to fill with duplicate pages. [S02–S05, S08]
The service business also matters. The business-context files identify installation and flatbeds as the two main offers, with service hours central to the business. The newer direction supplied for this project makes flatbeds the dominant marketing priority, not the only service the company should present. [I01]

1.3 Other changes worth making
Correct the easy credibility errors. The pages contain “prefect,” “Durbale,” and a sentence contrasting aluminum with “metal.” Aluminum is a metal. Replace broad durability language with specific, supported construction and use information. [S02, S03]
Make evidence useful at the decision point. A finished build should show the vehicle, the selected components, and why that configuration was chosen. Keep collaborative builds labeled as collaborations. Do not turn a partner endorsement into an apparently independent customer review. [S08]
Keep the offer separate from the permanent product story. A compact, current promotion can sit near the top. Full promotion terms can live on a dedicated offer page when a campaign needs one, with key restrictions still visible before inquiry or purchase. Do not hide material conditions to make the page look simpler.
Explain ownership and support. The clearance page describes three-month limited coverage for goods and installation workmanship. Bowen advertises lifetime craftsmanship coverage. Those are different promises, not a copywriting gap we can solve by inventing a stronger warranty. Have the business review what it can genuinely support, then explain the actual coverage plainly. [S02, S13]
Do not claim shorter delivery without a current installation window. In-stock product and an available install appointment are not the same thing. Likewise, Bowen's published production lead time does not prove sales volume or customer satisfaction. [S02, S13]

1.5 Research scope and limits
Firecrawl's URL map returned 300 URLs at the requested limit. That is an inventory sample, not a count of all pages or proof that all 300 URLs are indexed. The multi-page crawl was rate-limited, so the audit used individual Firecrawl retrievals and targeted public-page reads. The main hub, camper page, selector, Tacoma builder, consultation, contact, and Super Ute pages were directly retrieved with fresh Firecrawl requests. Other relevant service and company pages were inspected through public-page retrieval.
This review did not measure live conversion rates, ad performance, search rankings, Core Web Vitals, or the competitor's sales. A live interaction attempt was blocked by tool concurrency limits. Form submission, quote persistence, mobile tap behavior, and end-to-end CRM delivery still require staging tests. Embedded-frame blocking messages in the research environment were not treated as customer-facing website failures. Screenshot captures were not used to claim a completed visual or accessibility audit.

2.4 The buying journey
Offer two entry points: “Show me what fits my truck” and “Help me figure out the build.”
The self-directed path is vehicle, intended use, recommended starting configuration, customization, explained estimate, and quote request. The assisted path starts with the truck and the customer's goal, then lets the team recommend the next step.
After inquiry, distinguish qualification, any required paid design appointment, final quote approval, payment, scheduling, and installation. The business must approve which projects require the paid consultation.

Section 9. Answer the buying questions

Can I add accessories later? The system is modular, with compatible options that can be added as your needs change. Plan future additions early so the initial configuration does not create avoidable rework.
How much will the complete build cost? The answer depends on the platform, selected accessories, and installation scope. Use the builder to organize your choices, then request an itemized quote for your truck.
Does “in stock” mean you can install it immediately? Product availability and the installation calendar are separate. Confirm both before making plans.

4.1 Fix the product information once
Create a single approved record for each platform and option, whether in existing Shopify product data or a maintained specification layer.
Required fields: SKU and product name; model years and truck/bed fitment; dimensions; weight and exactly what that weight includes; documented structural rating and conditions; included components; compatible options; dependencies; price basis; installation scope; availability; applicable coverage; approver and last-reviewed date.
Price data must distinguish standard price, campaign price, product-only price, and any genuinely installed package price. Do not reuse old “from” prices from marketing blocks. A discount should be applied only to eligible SKUs under the approved offer rules.
The Super Ute's older builder exposes “Installed (Parts & Labor)” labels while the Tacoma page presents a body-only product list. Inventory and price reconciliation must cover both builder implementations, not only the newest template. [S05, S08]
4.2 Builder acceptance criteria
Every vehicle card opens the intended vehicle page, with consistent labeling and a valid fitment route.
The selected truck remains visible through configuration and inquiry.
Base-platform and option status are distinct; quantity and required dependencies are validated.
Adding or removing an item provides clear feedback and updates the correct summary.
The estimate label explains product, installation, discount, and tax treatment without mixing bases.
Navigation back to options does not unexpectedly lose the build. A save/share claim appears only if that function is implemented and tested.
The final request includes the chosen SKUs, quantities, source context, and the price snapshot used at inquiry.
Confirmation appears only after successful submission. Errors retain the build and provide an alternative contact route.
Desktop, mobile, keyboard navigation, zoom, form labels, focus, and overlays are tested in staging.
Analytics never records an option click as a completed lead, quote acceptance, or sale.
These are implementation tests to run, not behaviors this audit has already proven.

4.4 Measurement plan
Treat this as an extension and QA of the current tracking work, not a reason to rebuild analytics blindly.
Track the journey: relevant landing-page visit; builder start; vehicle selection; configuration review; successful inquiry; qualified opportunity; design consultation booked where applicable; quote issued; quote accepted; paid flatbed job.
Keep commercial definitions separate: A submitted form is a lead. A reviewed, feasible project is a qualified opportunity. An issued quote is not revenue. Use the team's agreed payment/order milestone for a sold job, and reconcile it with the actual record.
Useful dimensions: landing page, traffic source, campaign, device, truck, use case, new or returning visitor where available, and build/quote identifier. Keep personal information out of analytics event payloads.
Decision metric: More qualified flatbed opportunities and sold jobs at a viable acquisition cost, not simply more forms or time spent in the builder.
First compare before and after using equivalent periods and annotate promotions, traffic-mix changes, and inventory shifts. At low lead volumes, use recorded usability issues and sales feedback alongside rates; do not declare an A/B winner from a handful of conversions.

None of these decisions should stop correction of the verified bad links or placeholder text. They should stop unapproved promises from being published.

Final recommendation
Don't make the site bigger just to make it look more complete. Make the flatbed decision easier to understand and easier to act on.
The first win is a customer who recognizes the right setup, reaches the correct vehicle page, understands the estimate, and sends a useful request without having to solve the build alone. The site can then grow around real customer questions and completed projects instead of another layer of overlapping pages.
