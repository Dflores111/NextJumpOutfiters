# Next Jump: finishing the production experience

Prepared October 4, 2026. The current rebuild uses real existing Next Jump photography and the published Adventure Built walkaround. This brief specifies what still needs real-world inputs; it is not a claim that filming, modeling or deployment has happened.

## A small, useful media shoot

Shoot each scene in landscape with enough space for responsive crops. Capture a clean still before and after the action. Leave clear space above or beside the truck for headlines, and keep a second mobile framing with the entire truck readable.

1. **Hero / 8–12 seconds:** the Next Jump flatbed truck enters a forest-road frame and stops. Wide, steady composition; no rapid cuts or baked-in copy. Supply a silent WebM/MP4 loop and a sharp poster. The homepage offer and buttons remain immediately visible, with pause and static fallback.
2. **Storage / 6–10 seconds:** open an actual side box, remove the gear, close it. Show the access and scale. Identify the exact configuration; do not imply that every component is included.
3. **Tailgate and camper / 6–10 seconds each:** show the real rear opening, then the camper deployed. Keep load, mounting and use within the shop’s approved instructions.
4. **The shop / 10–15 seconds:** a technician fitting equipment, checking the installation, then walking the owner through it. Collect the technician’s public name and role before using an identifying caption.
5. **J. Scott / short interview:** why the shop exists, what he wants owners to experience, and what the team cares about when planning a truck. Record usable audio; provide captions/transcript.
6. **Customer story:** show the actual owner and truck. Ask what they wanted to change, why this setup, and what it now lets them do. Confirm publication permission and exact vehicle/equipment details.

Deliver camera originals plus web exports. Keep text, logos and captions editable. Do not use fabricated customer footage or generic stock trucks as project evidence.

## Four project features

Current: Super Ute collaboration, Dillon Collins’s 1993 F-250 lift, and clearly labeled Alaskan camper and Ford Ranger photographed setup examples. All four use actual published Next Jump media.

For a fuller camper customer story, supply the owner’s approved public name, exact truck/camper, before condition, work performed, several matched images and a customer-approved outcome. These are not available in the published source set. Any before/after must depict the same vehicle and verified work.

## Optional true 3D

The implemented explorer uses real photos, numbered feature points and manual selection. It does not simulate an unverified 3D configurator. To add accurate 3D, supply approved CAD/GLB models for each flatbed size and component, real dimensions and deterministic configuration/clearance rules. Test a compressed model on lower-powered phones with a complete photo fallback before deploying it.

## Commercial records to finish

The visible planner now says “Plan your build.” Before enabling prices or purchasing, reconcile the approved Shopify catalog with the proposed components:

- SKU/variant, platform size, included equipment and current price.
- Vehicle fitment, exclusions, mounting and load-rating review.
- Installation scope, shipping/tax handling and any required additional work.
- Availability/lead times and consultation, deposit, cancellation and warranty terms.

The existing live site contains differing promotional and starting-price claims. Those are not automatically applied to the planner. A firm quote requires an agreed scope.

## Final usability and performance session

Use one iPhone Safari and one Android Chrome device on mobile data. Ask a prospective buyer to find a relevant installation, inspect a real project, start a build and contact the team. Record confusion and missing information. Measure real loading/layout/interactivity on the deployed candidate; current layout checks and local builds are not Core Web Vitals certification.
