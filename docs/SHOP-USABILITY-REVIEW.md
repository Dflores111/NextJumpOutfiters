# Shop usability review — October 29, 2026

## Verdict

The connected pilot works in the tested paths. It is ready for a supervised team trial, not a claim that anyone can run the shop without training. This was an agent-led walkthrough, not a study with a first-time employee or an eighth-grade reader.

## Browser tasks actually completed

Using a fresh isolated demo on the published Site:

1. Opened NJO-DEMO-PREP, checked available stock, held six units and drafted one supplier order for the missing mounting kit.
2. Edited the draft order’s sample cost, recorded it as ordered, and received the one-unit delivery.
3. Opened its linked job, held the newly received unit, confirmed actual use and completed the job.
4. Checked Stock: mounting kits moved from 5 on hand to 6 after receipt, then 0 after using 6. Suspension kits ended at 5 on hand with 1 held for another job and 4 available.
5. Verified the completed job and stock history. Supplier orders were only demo records; nothing was sent or purchased.

In the revised local demo:

- Changed Weekender labor from 4 to 5 hours. A draft saved at $2,130 while the customer builder still showed $1,955. Both prices were labeled separately in the package list.
- Approved the fictional change through the review checks; the connected sample builder then showed $2,130.
- Prepared a shortage job and verified the success message explicitly said parts were still needed.
- Used the stock glossary and exact barcode 001234567890 to open the correct sample lighting part.

## Friction corrected

- Replaced the premature “Ready for the shop” success claim when parts are missing.
- Standardized visible job language around holding parts. Removed “allocated” from the ordinary job view.
- Explained stock numbers in a short optional disclosure.
- Added optional plain steps for package updates and quote-to-job work.
- Distinguished packages shown in the builder from private drafts, including separate builder and draft prices in both list and grid views.
- Used clearer cost, approval and task labels; kept advanced file tools out of the primary instructions.
- Corrected the inventory search field’s accessible name.

These changes preserve the existing approval, role, stock-count, shortage and transaction safeguards.

## What is still unproven or incomplete

- A real first-time employee has not completed the tasks without coaching. The language aims to be easy to read; no reading-grade certification is claimed.
- Production inspection showed 3 real Tacoma package drafts at revision 2 and no released package versions. The shop must approve actual parts, fitment, labor and prices before customers see those estimates.
- Opening stock must be physically counted and reconciled. This walkthrough used fictional stock.
- Actual staff access and the shop’s real receiving/install routine need an operational trial. Typed barcode lookup is tested; physical scanner hardware is not.
- Supplier orders are placed outside this app. Drafting or recording an order does not send it to a vendor.

## First-time employee trial

Ask one employee who did not build the system to complete these tasks without hints:

1. Find a part and explain on-hand, held and available stock.
2. Change a package, save the draft and explain which price customers see.
3. Prepare a quote with a shortage and find its supplier order.
4. Receive the delivery and hold the part for the linked job.
5. Complete the job and confirm what changed in stock.

Record where they pause, ask for help or predict the wrong result. Any unexplained critical stock or publishing step must be corrected before unsupervised use. Vehicle suitability and pricing approval remain work for qualified shop staff, even when the software is simple.
